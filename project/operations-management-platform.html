Concept case study · Enterprise / Public sector

Operations Management Platform.

A platform concept focused on bringing operational workflows into one clear experience, helping teams navigate information, manage tasks and make informed decisions.

Project
Operations Management Platform
Role
Product Designer / UX/UI Designer
Category
Enterprise / Public sector
Scope
Web platform, 5 modules

Problem

Teams juggle disconnected tools and manual steps, so information is scattered and decisions are slow.

My role

Product Designer / UX/UI Designer: research, workflows, information architecture, UI and design system.

Key decisions

One platform for five modules, a shared design system and a clear information architecture.

Outcome

A concept with defined success goals (adoption, speed, accuracy). No measured results are claimed.

Step 01

Context & challenge

Operational teams often work across disconnected tools and manual steps. This concept asks: what would it take to bring that work into one clear experience?

Challenge

Too many tools, too much manual work

Information is scattered, tasks are hard to track and decisions take longer than they should.

Goal

One platform teams actually want to use

Bring workflows together, reduce manual data entry and give people the clarity to act, in an interface that needs little training.

Step 02

Users & requirements

The platform serves people who plan, do and oversee operational work. Each group needs something different from the same information.

  • Team members

    See what needs attention today, update a task quickly and know what happens next.

  • Team leads

    Track progress across the team, spot blockers and keep work moving.

  • Managers and analysts

    Read reports and trends to make informed decisions.

  • Administrators

    Set up workflows, roles and permissions that stay clear and easy to review.

Step 03

Research & workflow mapping

Before any screens, the work is to understand how things happen today.

  1. 1

    Talk to stakeholders

    Understand goals, constraints and where the current process slows people down.

  2. 2

    Map the current workflow

    Follow a piece of work from request to completion and note every tool, hand-off and manual step.

  3. 3

    Find the friction

    Group the pain points: duplicated entry, unclear ownership, hard-to-find information.

  4. 4

    Prioritize

    Decide which problems the first version must solve and which can wait.

Step 04

Information architecture

One platform, five connected modules that share a single navigation and language.

Platform
  • Dashboard
  • Task management
  • Workflows
  • Reports & insights
  • Teams & access

  • Dashboard

    What needs attention today: open work, deadlines and key numbers.

  • Task management

    Every task has an owner, a status and a next step.

  • Workflows

    Repeatable processes set up once and followed the same way every time.

  • Reports & insights

    Information that is easy to read and act on.

  • Teams & access

    The right people see the right things.

Step 05

Wireframes

Low-fidelity layouts for each module, used to agree structure and hierarchy before visual design.

Step 06

UI & design system

Five design values guide every decision and keep a large platform feeling simple.

  • 01

    Clarity first

    Every screen answers one question: what do I do next?

  • 02

    Built for speed

    Fewer clicks between a task and getting it done.

  • 03

    Consistent everywhere

    One set of patterns, so people learn it once.

  • 04

    Open to everyone

    Readable, keyboard-friendly and accessible by default.

  • 05

    Made to scale

    A system that grows with the team, not against it.

Step 07

Prototype & validation

Interactive prototypes let stakeholders walk through the key flows before anything is built.

Prototype

Test the flows that matter most

Create a task, move it through a workflow, find it again and read the report that results.

Validation

Review with real users and stakeholders

Check usability and accessibility, note where people hesitate and refine the layouts and wording.

Step 08

Developer collaboration

A design is only useful if it can be built well, so engineers are involved early.

  • Shared components

    A library of documented components with their states and variants.

  • Clear specifications

    Spacing, type, colour and behaviour defined once and reused.

  • Accessibility notes

    Focus order, labels, contrast and keyboard behaviour written down.

  • Ongoing support

    Answer questions and review the built result against the design.

Step 09

Outcomes & goals

This is a concept case study, so these are the goals the design is built to achieve. Measured results will be added only when they can be verified.

5

Connected modules

Dashboard, tasks, workflows, reports and access in one platform.

5

Design values

One shared set of principles behind every screen.

1

Shared design system

Reusable components for consistent, buildable interfaces.

  • GoalFewer steps to finish a task
  • GoalLess manual data entry
  • GoalA clear view of work, at a glance
